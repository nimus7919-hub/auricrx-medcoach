Status: operational draft; assign contacts and rehearse before release. Applies to production and beta users, including US family testers.

1. Anyone discovering a suspected disclosure, loss, unauthorized access or service-provider breach immediately alerts the incident lead. Open a restricted incident record. Record discovery time in UTC, reporter, systems, evidence location, affected data and users, and every response decision. Discovery starts the US notification clock; do not wait for a completed investigation.
2. Contain promptly: restrict affected access, revoke exposed credentials/tokens, stop compromised processing and isolate services. Preserve logs, snapshots and access evidence with restricted access. Do not put health data or credentials into public tickets. Record containment actions and times.
3. Investigate with providers (Firebase/Google, Neon, Render, OpenAI and Stripe as relevant). Determine what happened, dates, countries, number of people, data categories, recipients and whether information was acquired without authorization. Document applicable-rule assessment with counsel; keep investigating while preparing notices.
4. Notify affected Mexican users immediately when a breach significantly affects their economic or moral rights. Give clear Spanish notice explaining the incident, affected information, protective steps, remediation and a working contact. This immediate-notice operational target follows the handoff and must not wait for a US deadline. Review LFPDPPP article 19 and implementing requirements with the legal reviewer.
5. For a reportable US breach under the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, notify affected individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery. Use required individual notice methods and required content: incident and discovery dates if known, data involved, protective steps, investigation/mitigation and contact methods. Provide clear English and any other required language/accessibility support.
6. If 500 or more individuals are affected, notify the FTC as soon as possible, concurrently with individual notification, within the same 60-day maximum. For fewer than 500, maintain a log and report to the FTC within 60 calendar days after the end of the calendar year. If 500 or more residents of a state or jurisdiction are affected, assess and make required prominent-media notification within the applicable deadline. Counsel must assess other federal/state duties and permitted law-enforcement delays; record the legal basis and revised deadline for any delay.
7. Track notice recipients, deadlines, delivery failures and substitute-notice obligations. Escalate unresolved delivery issues. Keep copies of notices and delivery evidence in the restricted incident record. Never promise that the maximum deadline is a waiting period.
8. Restore safely, validate controls and monitor for recurrence. Complete a root-cause review, assign fixes and due dates, update this plan and conduct a tabletop exercise. Document retention decisions and avoid retaining unnecessary health data in incident records.

Notice preparation checklist: facts and dates; affected data; what AuricRX has done; what the person can do; verified contact channel; language/accessibility review; legal review; delivery and regulator/media records. Use confirmed facts, clearly identify remaining uncertainty and provide updates.

Sources: [16 CFR Part 318, especially §§318.3–318.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-318) (checked November 2, 2026); [LFPDPPP](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFPDPPP.pdf) (Mexican notification instruction from the supplied legal handoff; official PDF could not be fetched during implementation).
